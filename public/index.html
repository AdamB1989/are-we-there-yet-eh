Act III — 2026 escalation

The latest Canada–U.S. trade actions and consequences.

Oct. 6, 2026

U.S. trade deficit widens to US$105.6 billion as imports hit US$420.8 billion

The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis and Census Bureau reported that the August goods-and-services trade deficit increased 13.7% to US$105.6 billion. Imports rose 4.3% to a record US$420.8 billion while exports rose 1.4% to US$315.2 billion. On a Census-basis goods measure, the U.S. deficit with Canada increased US$4.1 billion to US$7.1 billion, with U.S. exports to Canada at US$29.9 billion and imports from Canada at US$37.1 billion. The figures describe August trade flows; they do not by themselves establish that tariffs caused the wider overall deficit.

Primary source — U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis ↗
Oct. 6, 2026

Canada posts C$4.2 billion merchandise trade surplus in August

New August merchandise-trade data showed Canada’s surplus widening to C$4.2 billion, the highest in more than four years. Total exports rose 2.5% to C$77.91 billion while imports fell 2.0% to C$73.71 billion. Exports to the United States rose 8.1% ahead of new U.S. tariffs that took effect August 22, and Canada’s merchandise trade surplus with the U.S. reached C$11.2 billion, a 19-month high. Important context: these are August figures and include shipments made before the new tariffs took full effect, so they do not show a full month of post-tariff trade.

Verified reporting — Reuters ↗
Oct. 2, 2026

Canada and U.S. trade representatives meet at G20 — no new deal announced

Global Affairs Canada said International Trade Minister Maninder Sidhu met U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer on the margins of the G20 Trade Ministers’ Meeting in Milwaukee. Sidhu also met counterparts from Mexico, the European Union, India, China, Türkiye and other economies as Canada pursued trade cooperation, resilient supply chains and trade diversification. The official Canadian readout did not announce a new Canada–U.S. trade agreement or tariff concession resulting from the meeting.

Primary source — Global Affairs Canada ↗
Sept. 21, 2026

Bank of Canada says new U.S. tariffs could roughly halve Q4 growth

Governor Tiff Macklem said that if the new U.S. tariffs remain in place, Canadian economic growth could be roughly halved in the fourth quarter, to below 1%.

Primary source — Bank of Canada remarks ↗
Sept. 28, 2026

Stelco to idle Hamilton steel operations, affecting up to 500 workers

Reuters reported that Cleveland-Cliffs' Stelco will idle cold-rolled and coated steel operations in Hamilton beginning October 9, with up to 500 workers affected. Stelco attributed the move to U.S. tariffs and continued import pressure; that explanation is the company's stated position.

Verified reporting — Reuters ↗
Sept. 29, 2026

Scheduled U.S. import bans on selected Canadian goods take effect

The September 8 presidential proclamations took effect on September 29 for specified Canadian products, including packaged alcoholic beverages, selected dairy-related and whey products, and motorcycles over 800cc. The proclamations describe the measures as responses to alleged Canadian discrimination; that characterization is the U.S. administration's stated position.

Primary source — White House proclamation ↗
Sept. 29, 2026

Ottawa says it will press Cleveland-Cliffs to meet Stelco employment obligations

Reuters reported that Prime Minister Mark Carney said the federal government would do all it could to ensure Cleveland-Cliffs meets its legal employment obligations after the company announced production cuts affecting hundreds of Stelco workers in Hamilton. Carney did not announce a specific enforcement action at the time; the government's stated position is that it intends to hold the company to its existing commitments.

Verified reporting — Reuters ↗
Oct. 1, 2026

Canada lists Pacific Link as a project of national interest

The Government of Canada officially listed the proposed Pacific Link West Coast oil pipeline under Schedule 1 of the Building Canada Act. Ottawa says the project is intended to reduce reliance on the U.S. market by enabling an additional one million barrels of Canadian crude per day to reach Asian markets. Canada and Alberta say they will share equal ownership, with Indigenous communities offered a minimum 10% ownership interest. The designation starts an accelerated federal review and development process; it does not mean the pipeline is already approved, under construction, or operating.

Primary source — Prime Minister of Canada ↗ · Primary source — Major Projects Office ↗
Oct. 1, 2026

Ottawa directs $6.9 million in tariff-response support to Prince Edward Island

The Government of Canada announced more than $6.9 million through the Regional Tariff Response Initiative for 17 Prince Edward Island businesses and organizations. Ottawa says the funding is intended to help firms respond to tariff-related pressures, strengthen productivity and supply chains, and reach new customers. The package includes more than $260,000 for PEI Bag Co. for manufacturing equipment and digital technologies. The federal government says the PEI funding forms part of a broader $7.5 billion support package for workers and businesses affected by U.S. tariffs.

Primary source — Government of Canada / ACOA ↗
