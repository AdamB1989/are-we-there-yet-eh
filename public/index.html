Act I — Tariffs arrive

The opening round of Canada–U.S. trade measures in 2025.

Feb. 1, 2025

White House announces tariffs on Canadian imports

The White House announced a 25% additional tariff on imports from Canada, with a lower 10% rate for Canadian energy resources. The order linked the action to concerns about illicit drugs and border security; that rationale is the U.S. administration's stated position.

Primary source — White House fact sheet ↗
Feb. 1, 2025

Canada announces $155 billion in countermeasures

The Government of Canada announced 25% tariffs on $30 billion in U.S. goods, with a second list covering an additional $125 billion planned after consultation. Ottawa described the measures as a response to the U.S. tariffs.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗
Mar. 12, 2025

Canada answers U.S. steel and aluminum tariffs

Canada announced 25% reciprocal tariffs on $29.8 billion of U.S. goods after U.S. tariffs on steel and aluminum took effect. The Canadian package included steel products, aluminum products, and other U.S. goods.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗

Act II — The tariff map changes

Some 2025 measures were narrowed, delayed, exempted or removed as the dispute evolved.

Mar. 2025

USMCA-compliant goods receive tariff relief

The U.S. administration modified its Canada tariff action so that goods qualifying for preferential treatment under the USMCA could enter without the additional tariff, while non-qualifying goods remained subject to the order.

Primary source — White House presidential action ↗
Mar. 14, 2025

Canada publishes the steel-and-aluminum counter-tariff list

Canada implemented the previously announced 25% tariffs on $29.8 billion in U.S. goods, publishing the detailed product list and implementation guidance for importers.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗

Act III — 2026 escalation

The later round already on the site, now placed in a longer timeline.

Aug. 22, 2026

50% U.S. tariff takes effect

Canada's Department of Finance records that the United States imposed a 50% tariff on $27.6 billion of Canadian goods effective August 22. On August 25, Canada announced it would respond dollar-for-dollar and rate-for-rate.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗
Sept. 8, 2026

Canada's counter-tariffs take effect

Canada imposed 15%, 25%, and 50% tariffs on products covering $27.6 billion of U.S. imports. The government identified sectors including steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, plastics, and electronics.

Primary source — complete Canadian tariff list ↗
Sept. 8, 2026

Washington describes Canada's move as retaliation

On the same day, the U.S. Trade Representative said the President had used Section 338 authority to ban certain Canadian products and modify earlier tariff actions. USTR characterized Canada's measures as discriminatory and retaliatory; that characterization is the U.S. government's stated position.

Primary source — U.S. Trade Representative ↗
Sept. 8, 2026

U.S. proclamations schedule import bans on selected Canadian goods

Three presidential proclamations directed that specified Canadian motor-vehicle products, dairy products, and alcoholic beverages be excluded from U.S. importation beginning September 29, 2026. The proclamations state that covered goods imported before that date but not yet entered for consumption remain subject to the previously established 50% duty. The documents describe the action as a response to alleged Canadian discrimination; that characterization is the U.S. administration's stated position.

Primary source — White House motor-vehicle proclamation ↗ · Dairy proclamation ↗ · Alcohol proclamation ↗
Sept. 16, 2026

Canadian goods targeted in U.S. federal procurement

A presidential memorandum directed U.S. officials to identify Canadian-origin items in the federal civil procurement system and take legally permitted steps to remove or make them unavailable for purchase where warranted. The memorandum said the action responded to Canada's “Buy Canadian” procurement policy.

Primary source — White House memorandum ↗
Sept. 21, 2026

Bank of Canada says new U.S. tariffs could roughly halve Q4 growth

In remarks to the Halifax Partnership, Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said that if the new U.S. tariffs remain in place, Canadian economic growth could be roughly halved in the fourth quarter, to below 1%. He also said businesses are adapting by adjusting supply chains, exploring new markets and investing in new technologies.

Primary source — Bank of Canada remarks ↗
Sept. 28, 2026

Stelco to idle Hamilton steel operations, affecting up to 500 workers

Reuters reported that Cleveland-Cliffs' Stelco will idle cold-rolled and coated steel operations in Hamilton beginning October 9, with up to 500 workers affected. Stelco attributed the move to the effects of U.S. tariffs on Canadian steel and continued high import volumes; that explanation is the company's stated position. The company said affected Hamilton workers would be offered employment opportunities at its Nanticoke facility.

Verified reporting — Reuters ↗
