Act I — Tariffs arrive

The opening round of Canada–U.S. trade measures in 2025.

Feb. 1, 2025

White House announces tariffs on Canadian imports

The White House announced a 25% additional tariff on imports from Canada, with a lower 10% rate for Canadian energy resources. The order linked the action to concerns about illicit drugs and border security; that rationale is the U.S. administration's stated position.

Primary source — White House fact sheet ↗
Feb. 1, 2025

Canada announces $155 billion in countermeasures

The Government of Canada announced 25% tariffs on $30 billion in U.S. goods, with a second list covering an additional $125 billion planned after consultation. Ottawa described the measures as a response to the U.S. tariffs.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗
Mar. 12, 2025

Canada answers U.S. steel and aluminum tariffs

Canada announced 25% reciprocal tariffs on $29.8 billion of U.S. goods after U.S. tariffs on steel and aluminum took effect.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗

Act II — The tariff map changes

Some 2025 measures were narrowed, delayed, exempted or removed as the dispute evolved.

Mar. 2025

USMCA-compliant goods receive tariff relief

The U.S. administration modified its Canada tariff action so that goods qualifying for preferential treatment under the USMCA could enter without the additional tariff.

Primary source — White House presidential action ↗
Mar. 14, 2025

Canada publishes the steel-and-aluminum counter-tariff list

Canada implemented the previously announced 25% tariffs on $29.8 billion in U.S. goods.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗

Act III — 2026 escalation

The later round already on the site, now placed in a longer timeline.

Aug. 22, 2026

50% U.S. tariff takes effect

Canada's Department of Finance records that the United States imposed a 50% tariff on $27.6 billion of Canadian goods effective August 22.

Primary source — Department of Finance Canada ↗
Sept. 8, 2026

Canada's counter-tariffs take effect

Canada imposed 15%, 25%, and 50% tariffs on products covering $27.6 billion of U.S. imports.

Primary source — complete Canadian tariff list ↗
Sept. 8, 2026

U.S. proclamations schedule import bans on selected Canadian goods

Three presidential proclamations directed that specified Canadian products be excluded from U.S. importation beginning September 29, 2026. The documents describe the action as a response to alleged Canadian discrimination; that characterization is the U.S. administration's stated position.

Primary source — White House proclamation ↗
Sept. 16, 2026

Canadian goods targeted in U.S. federal procurement

A presidential memorandum directed U.S. officials to identify Canadian-origin items in the federal civil procurement system.

Primary source — White House memorandum ↗
Sept. 21, 2026

Bank of Canada says new U.S. tariffs could roughly halve Q4 growth

Governor Tiff Macklem said that if the new U.S. tariffs remain in place, Canadian economic growth could be roughly halved in the fourth quarter, to below 1%.

Primary source — Bank of Canada remarks ↗
Sept. 28, 2026

Stelco to idle Hamilton steel operations, affecting up to 500 workers

Reuters reported that Cleveland-Cliffs' Stelco will idle cold-rolled and coated steel operations in Hamilton beginning October 9, with up to 500 workers affected. Stelco attributed the move to U.S. tariffs and continued import pressure; that explanation is the company's stated position.

Verified reporting — Reuters ↗
Sept. 29, 2026

Scheduled U.S. import bans on selected Canadian goods take effect

The September 8 presidential proclamations took effect on September 29 for specified Canadian products, including packaged alcoholic beverages, selected dairy-related and whey products, and motorcycles over 800cc. The proclamations describe the measures as responses to alleged Canadian discrimination; that characterization is the U.S. administration's stated position.
